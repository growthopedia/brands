How to use it

Three jobs in one app: brand outreach (below), links and creators (further down). One card per brand on the site, ranked by how likely they are to reply. The % is an estimate: small and mid-size brands answer more than big corporate ones, a partnerships or affiliate email beats a press inbox, which beats a general inbox or a contact form, and brands that already run an affiliate program want publishers. Researching a brand sharpens its number. "Value" (more products, pricier gear, big-ticket categories) is the second sort. For each brand you track five asks: image permission, affiliate program, samples, a TikTok/social feature, and spec data. Nothing ever sends without you pressing Approve & send.

The order that works

Now, before launch

  1. Today → Research next 10. Claude searches each brand's site for a published PR, partnerships or affiliate email, their contact form, affiliate network, and parent company. It never guesses addresses. About 5 to 10 cents per brand.
  2. Open a brand → Write email. First email: image permission + spec data, or image permission + affiliate question. Two asks max.
  3. No inbox yet? Press Copy, send it through their contact form (or any inbox you choose), then I sent it myself. The brand moves to contacted and gets a follow-up date.
  4. Samples and TikTok are easy yeses pre-launch. Traffic numbers aren't needed for those.

At launch

  1. Site on its real domain with an affiliate disclosure, privacy policy, and real content. Affiliate networks review your site before approving, and a "coming soon" site usually gets declined.
  2. Join the networks in the Affiliates tab: Impact, Awin (includes ShareASale), CJ, AvantLink, Rakuten, Amazon Associates. One network account unlocks many brands.
  3. Then apply to each brand's program inside its network and log it (Applied on affiliate network).
  4. Update Settings → About the site so emails stop saying "launching soon".

Store brands

Brands marked store brand (up&up, Parent's Choice, IKEA, Crate & Kids, Pottery Barn Kids) belong to a retailer. You won't get a brand team; the retailer's own affiliate program is the way in (Target and Walmart run theirs through Impact).

Image permission

Product photos are copyrighted. Two ways to use them legally:

  • Written permission from the brand. An email saying which images, where, and any credit line. The day they say yes, open the brand → Log permission and paste their exact words. If their reply is vague ("sure!"), use Write an answer: Claude confirms the terms back to them in one sentence so you have a clear record.
  • Affiliate network feeds. Once you're approved in a brand's program, the network's product feed and creatives usually come with permission to use those images for promoting the products. Read each program's terms.

Only use a brand's images while the Image rights tab shows them active. If a brand takes it back, press They took it back and pull the images. Export the log now and then and keep it with the original emails. This isn't legal advice; a lawyer can review your standard wording once.

Rules that keep you out of trouble

  • Never trade placement, scores, or "best" rankings for anything. The emails say comparisons stay independent.
  • Free samples and affiliate links must be disclosed (FTC). Say "gifted" or "#ad" in TikToks of free products, and keep the affiliate disclosure on every page.
  • Every email includes your mailing address and an unsubscribe line (CAN-SPAM). Sending stays off until the address is set.
  • "Unsubscribe" or "remove me" replies put the brand on do-not-contact forever. Bounced addresses get cleared so you can find a new contact.
  • Start with a daily limit around 15 on a new domain. A new inbox that blasts email lands in spam.

Turning on sending (once the domain and LLC exist)

  1. Create the inbox on the new domain (Google Workspace, e.g. hello@yourdomain.com).
  2. In Google Admin, turn on DKIM. Add SPF (v=spf1 include:_spf.google.com ~all) and DMARC (_dmarc → v=DMARC1; p=none; rua=mailto:hello@yourdomain.com) to the domain's DNS.
  3. console.cloud.google.com, signed in as the new inbox → new project → enable Gmail API → OAuth consent screen Internal → Credentials → OAuth client ID → Web application → Authorized redirect URI https://brands.cribpicks.com/oauth/callback.
  4. App → Settings: sending address, mailing address, paste the Client ID and secret → Connect Gmail → sign in as the new inbox.

Replies

If the inbox is Google Workspace and Gmail is connected, the app reads replies to its own emails every hour by itself. Nothing else to set up.

Forwarding covers everything else: brands answering a contact form, replies to emails you sent by hand, or an inbox that isn't Gmail. Cloudflare Email Routing hands each message to this app, which files it on the right brand (by thread, contact email, then the brand's domain) and forwards a copy to you.

  1. The domain must use Cloudflare DNS. Cloudflare → the domain → Email → Email Routing → enable.
  2. Destination addresses → add and verify the inbox you want copies in.
  3. Routing rules → custom address → action Send to a Worker → brands.
  4. App → Settings → Forward incoming mail to = that verified inbox.

A domain's mail goes to one place. If Google Workspace runs the inbox, Google owns the domain's mail, so put the Cloudflare route on a subdomain instead (e.g. replies@in.yourdomain.com) and add a Gmail filter that auto-forwards brand mail there. Mail the app can't match lands in Replies → Unmatched for you to file.

How emails are checked

Every email Claude finds is checked before it's saved, and you see a badge next to it:

  • ✓ Verified: the address is written on the page Claude cited, the domain receives mail, and it's on the brand's own site domain (or it's a creator's own address).
  • ~ Likely: on the brand's domain but missing from the cited page, on a look-alike domain, or from a page more than two years old. Usually fine; give it a look.
  • ⚠ Unverified: couldn't confirm it (page wouldn't load, different domain, or entered by you). Use the Find buttons or their contact form.
  • ✕ Can't receive mail: the domain doesn't exist or has no mail servers. Claude's suggestion isn't saved, and the app won't send to it.

Brands → Check emails checks everything saved before this existed; Check again on a card re-checks one. No check can promise a mailbox is still active (only sending can), so a bounce still happens now and then. Bounced addresses are blocked for good and cleared from the card.

Links (backlinks)

The Links tab finds sites that might link to the comparison site, writes the pitch, and checks that won links stay live. Pitches share the Drafts tab, the daily send limit, reply tracking and forwarding with brand emails.

Timing

Links only help the domain they point at. Until the real domain is live and Settings → Site address points to it, build and research the list but hold the pitches. Once the domain is set, put it in Settings and every new pitch uses it.

Tactics, best first for a new site

  • Resource pages: "new parent resources" and registry resource lists. A free, no-sign-up comparison tool is exactly what they list.
  • Parent groups & local orgs: hospitals, childbirth educators, family resource centers, New York family magazines. Being a local parent helps here.
  • Guest posts: useful articles for parenting blogs (how to compare fold sizes, which car seat specs matter). Slower, but you control the content.
  • Partner brands: brands that already gave you image permission. Open the brand → Ask them for a link.
  • Gear roundups & journalists: lower odds. Offer spec data, never ask them to change their picks.
  • Unlinked mentions: after launch, pages that name the site without linking. Highest odds of all.
  • Competitor links: export a competitor's referring pages from Ahrefs or Semrush and use Import list. UTF-16 Ahrefs files work as-is.

Rules (Google's spam policies)

  • Never pay, send free products, or trade links for a link. The pitches never offer any of that, and Claude politely declines paid placement requests.
  • Never ask for "dofollow" or mention SEO in a pitch. Relevant, editorial links are the ones that count.
  • Guest posts: write genuinely useful pieces with at most one natural link back. Mass guest posting with keyword-rich anchors is a link scheme.

Won links

When a site adds your link, open it → Mark won & check link with the page address. The app confirms the link is in the page, records the anchor text and whether it's follow, nofollow, sponsored or ugc, and re-checks it about once a day. Lost links show at the top of Won links. Pages that build their content with JavaScript can hide a real link from this check; open the page to confirm.

Authority is optional: paste a DR or DA from Ahrefs, Moz or Semrush. Very high-authority sites score lower on "likely to answer" because they rarely reply to small sites.

Creators

The Creators tab (under More on a phone) finds small YouTubers, TikTokers and Instagrammers (and bloggers if you pick Blog) who posted registry videos or reviews of gear on the site. Claude matches the products they showed to your product pages, so each note points them at the exact comparison page for what they featured.

How to use it

  1. Find creators: pick registries, a category, a brand or one product, and a platform. Up to 10 new creators per run.
  2. Find contact: Claude looks for a published business email (YouTube About, bio, link-in-bio). No email? Write a DM instead: copy it, send it from your account, press I sent it.
  3. What to ask (each optional for them): check the page and share it if it helps their viewers; be featured as "Recommended by creators" on that product page; have their video shown there.
  4. When they say yes, open them → Log what they said yes to and paste their exact words. Claude's answer to their reply also confirms the terms back to them so the record is clear.

Creator sizes

Small is under 25k followers, Medium 25k to 250k, Large 250k and up. Pick a size and how many (15 to 60) when you search; size is a lean, not a strict filter, so you see everyone found, labeled. After each search the app looks up every new creator's follower count and notes where it saw it (hover the number). Counts for small TikTok accounts often aren't public to search, so those show Followers unknown: type the number in from their profile and the size and % update. Use the size chips above the list to filter.

Small creators answer far more often than big ones, so the % favors smaller accounts. Large creators usually work through a manager and expect to be paid; pitch them once the site is live and you have something to offer.

Rules for featuring creators

  • Only show a creator on the site once their yes is logged here. Using someone's name, photo or video to promote the site without written consent is not allowed in New York (Civil Rights Law sections 50 and 51) and many other states.
  • Never write or edit their quote. Show only words they gave you, and never imply they endorse more than they said.
  • If you ever give a creator anything (free product, payment, affiliate commission), they have to disclose it to their viewers (FTC endorsement rules), and the site should note it next to their feature too.
  • If they ask to be removed, press They asked to remove it and take it off the site the same day.
  • Wait for the real domain before asking them to share a link, so the link they post points to the right place.

The list of everything you're cleared to show is at Settings → Exports → Creator features (for the site), ready for a "Recommended by creators" section on product pages.

Grow (@thecribpicks)

Accounts to follow and comment on, post ideas from the product list, and a playbook. The app can't follow anyone for you: TikTok and Instagram don't allow it, and automated following gets accounts restricted. You open each account, follow by hand, and press Followed.

  1. Add brands + creators I already have pulls in brand Instagram accounts and everyone on the Creators tab.
  2. Find accounts searches for parenting, expert (CPSTs, lactation, sleep), local New York and creator accounts on one platform, leaning toward 1k to 100k followers.
  3. Today gives up to 15 to follow (creators you're pitching first, then small accounts in the niche) and up to 8 you follow but haven't commented on in a week.
  4. Post ideas writes 7 short-video ideas at a time: hook, beats, caption, hashtags and the Cribpicks page to show. Plan them on a date, then mark them posted.

Follow-backs are a small part of growth. Videos are most of it: see Playbook in the tab.

Updating the product list

Settings → Import CSV with the same columns (Section, Type, Brand, Product, Model, List price (USD), Status, Past recall, Maker page, Site page). New brands get added and ranked; existing brands keep their contacts, notes, emails and permissions.
